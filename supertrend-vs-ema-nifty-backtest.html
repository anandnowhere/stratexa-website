Supertrend vs 9/21 EMA: Which Strategy Survived 5 Years of Nifty Volatility?

October 20, 2026 Stratexa Quant Desk 8 min read Strategy Teardown

Trend following in Indian equity indices is one of the most widely discussed trading methodologies. Yet, retail traders frequently debate between two classic technical engines: the Supertrend (10, 3) indicator versus the classic 9 & 21 Exponential Moving Average (EMA) crossover.

To move past theoretical discussions and YouTube anecdotes, our quantitative research desk conducted a backtest spanning 1,200 trading sessions across Nifty 50 on the 15-minute timeframe. Here is the verified breakdown of win rates, maximum drawdowns, and profit factors.

1. Strategy Parameters & Backtest Architecture

Both models were tested under identical execution constraints:

  • Underlying Asset: Nifty 50 Futures / Synthetic Index Options.
  • Timeframe: 15-Minute Candles (09:15 AM to 03:15 PM IST).
  • Slippage & Brokerage: 0.05% slippage per turn + standard STT and exchange fees.
  • Exit Rules: EOD square-off at 03:15 PM or reverse signal trigger.

2. Why Supertrend Outperformed in Volatile Regimes

The core advantage of Supertrend over standard moving averages lies in its integration of the Average True Range (ATR). While EMA crossovers react purely to price distance, Supertrend continuously adapts its stop-loss line based on prevailing market volatility.

During sudden volatility spikes (such as India VIX crossing 16), moving averages frequently generate delayed crossovers, resulting in whipsaws where traders buy the top and sell the bottom. Conversely, Supertrend's ATR trailing band widens during choppy sessions and tightens during directional impulses, preserving capital.

3. The Psychological Trap of Low Win Rates

Notice that both strategies have win rates below 45%. This is the mathematical reality of authentic trend-following systems: they take frequent small losses during sideways consolidations and generate their entire annual alpha from 15-20 outlier trending days.

This is precisely where discretionary traders fail: after experiencing 4 consecutive small stop-losses during a range-bound week, a human trader hesitates and skips the 5th trade—which inevitably turns out to be the 300-point trending day that recovers all drawdowns. Systematic algorithmic execution removes this emotional friction completely.
